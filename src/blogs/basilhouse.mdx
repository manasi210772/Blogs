## <u>Introduction</u>

Today, I visited **Basil House**, a vegetarian restaurant at **The Walk, Hiranandani Estate**, for lunch.

Since it was a weekday, there wasn't much of a rush. The first thing I noticed was the ambience. The restaurant was very aesthetic and clean. Even the dishes, bowls and plates in which they served the food were elegant and added to the overall dining experience.

However, when we received the menu, I realised that it didn't have as much variety as I had expected. Most of the dishes were similar to the regular vegetarian food you would generally find at a Punjabi restaurant.

Nevertheless, we decided to try something different.

## <u>What We Ordered</u>

We ordered three dishes:

- **Dhaniya Tamatar Shorba**
- **Tofu Chilli**
- **Singaporean Noodles**

## <u>Dhaniya Tamatar Shorba</u>

In English, **Dhaniya Tamatar** translates to coriander and tomato.

This was the first dish we tried. The quantity was comparatively smaller than what you generally get at other restaurants.

As for the taste, it was more or less similar to the classic tomato soup that we usually get at restaurants. It had a sweet-and-sour flavour, leaning more towards the sour side, along with a slight kick of spice.

The soup contained coriander seed powder, and coriander leaves were also used as a garnish.

Overall, it was a very familiar yet delicious flavour. I really enjoyed it!

## <u>Tofu Chilli</u>

Both paneer and tofu were available on the menu. However, I wanted to try something new, so I decided to order **Tofu Chilli**.

The sauces, spices and overall taste were exactly like the Indo-Chinese food we usually have. The quantity was also perfectly sufficient for two people.

But there was one major disappointment — **the tofu itself**.

The tofu was very dry and compact from the inside. It wasn't moist, and the flavours of the chilli masala hadn't really penetrated into it. This made the tofu quite hard to bite into.

On the other hand, the Indo-Chinese flavours were absolutely on point. The dish had a nice spicy kick, and I really enjoyed the combination of sauces and spices.

If only the tofu had been softer and more flavourful on the inside, this dish could have been much better.

## <u>Singaporean Noodles</u>

This was my **first time trying Singaporean Noodles**, so I was quite curious about how they would taste.

The noodles were served with vegetables such as red capsicum, spring onion and cabbage.

One thing I immediately noticed was the generous amount of black pepper. Almost every strand of noodle had tiny grains of black pepper sticking to it. This made me wonder how thoroughly they must have tossed the noodles with the pepper!

However, there was no curry or gravy served alongside the noodles. Instead, we were given a pickle that tasted quite similar to our regular mango pickle.

Because the noodles were served without any gravy, they felt very dry.

After a few bites, I noticed that the black pepper was actually sticking to my palate. It started absorbing more moisture from my mouth, making the overall eating experience a little unpleasant.

The black pepper also became the dominant flavour of the dish.

Interestingly, having the noodles with the pickle made the experience slightly better. The pickle added some extra flavours and prevented me from experiencing flavour fatigue from the dominant black pepper taste.

Overall, I liked the idea of trying something new, but the dryness and excessive black pepper made the dish less enjoyable than I had expected.

## <u>Final Thoughts</u>

Overall, I had a decent experience at Basil House.

The ambience was beautiful, the presentation was elegant, and the Dhaniya Tamatar Shorba was definitely my favourite dish of the day.

Although the Tofu Chilli and Singaporean Noodles had some shortcomings, I still enjoyed exploring a few different dishes.

And I haven't completely crossed this restaurant off my list!

I would love to visit Basil House again because there are two dishes I am particularly interested in trying:

- **Jhol Nepali Momos**
- **Ghee Toasted Ice Cream**

Hopefully, these two turn out to be even better!
